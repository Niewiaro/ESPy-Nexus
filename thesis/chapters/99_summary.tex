\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Summary}
\chapter*{Summary}
\endgroup

The aim of this thesis was to investigate empirically the performance limits of wireless communication in real-time embedded systems and to identify the design trade-offs that should be considered when selecting a~communication protocol.
To achieve this aim, ESPy-Nexus, an original Hardware-in-the-Loop (HIL) research platform, was designed, implemented, and evaluated.
The platform combines a~real ESP32 device under test with a~Python-based control and measurement environment and a~web-based analytical interface.
This architecture made it possible to execute repeatable experiments while preserving the influence of the target processor, memory, communication peripherals, protocol stacks, operating-system scheduling, and network infrastructure.

The embedded part of the system was implemented in C++ for the ESP32 platform.
It uses FreeRTOS tasks assigned to the two processor cores to separate control-plane activities from data-plane processing.
The control plane receives commands from the host computer, manages the test state, and initiates the selected communication strategy.
The data plane provides a~common interface for several transmission methods: text and binary serial communication, UDP, TCP, and WebSockets.
The binary formats use application-level framing so that packet boundaries can be identified in byte streams.
Measurement records contain packet identifiers and reception timestamps generated by the ESP32.
An external PSRAM buffer allows the device to store a~large number of records before transferring them to the host computer.
A~binary memory dump was used to reduce the communication overhead during result acquisition.

The host-side software was developed in Python.
It separates experiment orchestration, device control, data transmission, raw-data acquisition, and batch analysis.
The test engine generates a~configurable test matrix based on the selected topology, protocol, transmission frequency, payload size, packet count, drain time, and cooldown period.
The collected transmission and reception records are combined using packet identifiers and stored in SQLite databases.
The analytical pipeline then transforms raw timestamps into comparable quality-of-service metrics.
These metrics include Packet Delivery Ratio (PDR), duplicate counts, inter-arrival-time jitter, coefficient of variation, burst-loss length, blackout time, goodput, out-of-order events, relative queuing delay, and a~clock-drift indicator.
Deterministic mock scenarios were also used to verify the interpretation of losses, duplicates, irregular inter-arrival times, reordering, and increasing delay.

The experimental matrix covered the \texttt{NONE}, \texttt{AP\_ESP}, \texttt{AP\_PC}, and \texttt{AP\_ROUTER} network topologies, payload sizes from 4 B~to 1024 B, and transmission frequencies from 1 Hz to 10,000 Hz.
The results show that no single protocol is optimal for all evaluation criteria.
The serial interface can provide a~useful baseline, but its performance deteriorates rapidly as the payload grows.
For \texttt{SERIAL\_BIN} with a~4 B~payload, PDR remained equal to 100 percent up to 5600 Hz and decreased to 93.4 percent at 5700 Hz.
With a~1024 B~payload, the first losses appeared at 40 Hz, while at 50 Hz PDR decreased to 1.1 percent and the maximum measured burst blackout reached 7.4 s.

TCP and WebSockets preserved the integrity and ordering of the tested stream, and TCP maintained a~PDR of 100 percent in the analysed cases.
This reliability was accompanied by substantial temporal degradation under heavy load.
For TCP in the \texttt{AP\_PC} topology, with a~4 B~payload at 3900 Hz, the coefficient of variation reached approximately 3000 percent and the maximum relative delay was about 2.23 s.
The result demonstrates that complete delivery does not imply timely delivery and that PDR alone is insufficient for evaluating a~real-time communication channel.

UDP offered a~different trade-off.
In the \texttt{AP\_ROUTER} topology with a~1024 B~payload, the measured goodput reached approximately 28.03 Mbps, while PDR at this load was about 65 percent.
The results therefore indicate that UDP can be appropriate when an application prioritizes current data and bounded delay over the delivery of every packet.
The topology also had a~significant effect on the results.
For UDP and a~1024 B~payload, the first observed losses occurred at approximately 100 Hz for \texttt{AP\_PC}, 128 Hz for \texttt{AP\_ROUTER}, and 300 Hz for \texttt{AP\_ESP}.
At high frequencies, this initial ranking could change: for 16 B~payloads and the 9000-10,000 Hz range, the average PDR was approximately 91.6 percent for \texttt{AP\_PC} and 26.6 percent for \texttt{AP\_ESP}.

The main project objective was achieved to a~high degree.
ESPy-Nexus provides an operational and extensible environment for testing a~real embedded device, automating experiments, acquiring data, calculating metrics, and presenting aggregated results.
At the same time, the conclusions must be limited to the tested hardware, firmware, network configurations, and operating conditions.
The use of a~general-purpose operating system on the host prevents the platform from being treated as a~deterministic real-time traffic source.
Active waiting can reduce timing variation, but it cannot remove the influence of scheduling, drivers, operating-system buffers, or network devices.
Relative delay and clock drift should therefore be interpreted as diagnostic indicators dependent on the adopted model rather than as direct measurements of a~single physical phenomenon.

The results lead to several practical recommendations.
UDP should be considered for closed-loop control and telemetry when the application can tolerate losses and requires current data with limited delay; it should be supplemented with packet identification, freshness checks, and an application-level response to missing data.
TCP or WebSockets are more suitable for configuration transfer, files, firmware updates, and other applications in which integrity and complete delivery are essential, but their delay must be controlled when they are used near real-time systems.
UART remains useful for short commands, configuration, diagnostics, and baseline measurements, while large payloads require particular attention to throughput and buffering.
The topology should always be evaluated together with the intended frequency and payload range rather than selected from low-load results alone.

Further work should include experiments with other ESP32 modules, network adapters, routers, and operating systems; comparison with a~real-time or hardware traffic generator; clock synchronization using methods such as PTP; longer and repeated measurements for estimating uncertainty; and additional instrumentation of temperature, processor load, memory usage, buffer states, and lower protocol layers.
Such extensions would improve the separation of the causes of packet loss and delay and would support automatic acceptance criteria tailored to a~specific application.
